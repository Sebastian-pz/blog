Through a contact at university, a regional office of a multinational asked me to validate job profiles they kept in Excel. I call it Company A.

They were migrating platforms. Each employee's record was spread across several workbooks and systems, and the deadline was short.

The job was to consolidate that data and check, in VBA (Visual Basic for Applications), whether the person met the requirements of the role.

If they did not, the procedure notified human resources and placed them on the incomplete-profile list. The section lead received that list and decided whether the profile had to be completed. If they did meet it, they stayed on the up-to-date list, with no further action.

The two lists produced counts: sectors with the most gaps, requirements that failed most often, and roles with the most people outside the profile.

Each validation was stored in a OneDrive folder and on the new platform. The employee record lived in one place.

The calculation was simple. The volume was not: human resources opened the files by hand. Building one employee's record took more than an hour, workbook by workbook and system by system. With the procedure, the lookup is by name, national ID, or email.

That left them an inventory of profile compliance, which is what an audit asks to review. They did the follow-up. The program makes a gap visible. It does not close it.
