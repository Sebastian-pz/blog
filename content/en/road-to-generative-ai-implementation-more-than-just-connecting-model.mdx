I enrolled in Microsoft's Professional Foundations of Generative AI course to sort out what sits behind these tools. Calling a pretrained model and displaying the response is the visible part. The work is in the data, the input, the cost of each inference, and the bias of the output.

## Training a model and calling an API are different jobs

The course puts together families that are not integrated the same way. A GAN or a variational autoencoder is trained: it needs a large dataset, curated and balanced across classes. If a portrait generator sees many more images of one demographic group than of others, the output repeats that imbalance.

A transformer consumed through an API, such as GPT or DALL·E, is a different contract. You do not train the model. You pass an input and pay for inference. Treating both cases as "download the model and use it" mixes training with integration.

## The prompt is the input

In a generative model, the prompt is the input that conditions the output. "Generate an image" and a prompt that fixes subject, framing, and constraints do not produce the same result. A small change in the text changes the generation.

Tightening the prompt raises the number of usable responses and usually cuts retries and output tokens. That lowers the cost of the call. It does not change the model's internal compute cost, and it does not replace fine-tuning.

## From a notebook to real traffic

Google Colab and Jupyter are enough to try something. Serving other people's requests is a different account: memory, compute, and what happens when more than one request arrives at once.

Fine-tuning is not the step every product takes. Tuning hyperparameters on your own dataset makes sense when the pretrained model does not cover the case. When it does, the work is specifying the prompt, evaluating outputs, and deciding what gets shown. Tuning out of habit leaves another training run to maintain.

## Bias comes from the data and from design decisions

In the course I ran into the line "the biggest bias in AI is the human inferiority complex." I do not share it. Wariness that a model might replace a task is a labor question. The bias that reaches the user comes from the training set and from whoever labels, filters, or defines the loss function.

In a GAN, the generator tries to produce samples the discriminator classifies as real. If the discriminator was trained on biased data, it learns that distribution and the generator reproduces it.

## What has to be decided when integrating one

A transformer already generates text, images, and video that are hard to tell apart from human work. Integrating one means choosing the model, the data, the input, the cost per inference, and how the bias of the output is reviewed. The course is useful for naming those pieces.
